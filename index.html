About Me

I am a BSc Industrial Physics graduate (Electronics & IT Application) from Covenant University, Nigeria, where I graduated with a CGPA of 4.16/5.00. My academic formation spans electromagnetics, signal processing, atmospheric physics, and computational methods.

My research examines how climate-driven changes in atmospheric water vapour affect microwave radio wave communication in Abuja, Nigeria — applying ITU-R P.453-14 formulations to a decade of meteorological data and validating results against standard propagation models.

Beyond signal propagation, I develop machine learning systems for energy forecasting — including an ongoing Nigeria Grid Energy Demand Forecasting project using LSTM neural networks and Random Forest models trained on NERC grid data.

I was a selected participant in the Climate Change AI Virtual Summer School 2026, where I completed tutorials in statistical downscaling, optimal power flow with AI, tsunami alert prediction, and bioacoustic monitoring — all notebooks publicly available on GitHub.

Research

Effect of Increased Atmospheric Water Vapour due to Climate Change on Radio Wave Communication in Abuja, North-Central Nigeria
BSc Thesis · Covenant University · March–August 2025 · DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22759087

This study applies the ITU-R P.453-14 formulation to a decade of meteorological observations for Abuja, Nigeria (2015–2024) to compute monthly and seasonal profiles of water vapour pressure, saturation vapour pressure, and enhancement factors. A Multiple Linear Regression model validated against the ITU-R P.453-14 propagation standard achieved an R² of 95.94%, identifying atmospheric water vapour as the primary predictor of signal attenuation. Seasonal peaks in April–May present the highest risk to frequencies above 10 GHz, with implications for 5G and satellite link reliability in West African tropical climates.
